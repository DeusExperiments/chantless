1. Audience and pain

Pain #1 · user-confirmedDecided

Three audience tiers were mapped. A few propositions were explicitly set aside as failing the bar, while many others were considered and simply not selected. The strategy settled builder-first on harness users whose feedback does not stick, with the end-user experience kept as the pass/fail test; you confirmed the repeat-correction pain as number one.

pain = "I already corrected this — why must I catch it again?"
Decided
  • Builder-first; end-user experience is the acceptance test.
  • Initial audience: agent-harness users whose corrections do not stick.
  • Target pain: a rejected approach returning in different code or wording.
Evidence
  • Firsthand reports: 11 Aug (rules recited then violated), 4 Sep (12 skipped tracker updates), 29 Sep (repeated "continue"), 3 Jul (rejected SVG approach returned via SVG→PNG). Complaints, not prevalence data.
Explicitly set aside ("do not revive unchanged")
  • "Smaller AI", "offline coding", "cheaper automation" — familiar benefits, no distinct edge.
  • Send someone your cooking skill — skipped learning mechanism, demand and client software.
  • "We manufacture a finished capability" — useful offer, not enough differentiation alone (Distil labs).
  • "Put intelligence inside ordinary controls" — local action models already exist.
  • "The demo looks impressive, therefore OnlySkills wins."
Considered, not selected
  • Keyboard needing less correcting; "change your mind by continuing to type".
  • Voice shopping cart — overlap with on-device voice ordering found.
  • Jointly compiled skill collection; tested configuration family (down/up/sideways).
  • "Make the change once"; field-visit register; Indian-language errand assistant; documents with semantic functions.
  • Argument grounding (BFCL), scope checking, receipt totals, document classification — superseded by the repeat-correction pain.
